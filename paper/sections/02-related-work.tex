\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Integrated scheduling with transport}

Brandimarte~\cite{brandimarte1993} introduced a set of flexible job shop
benchmarks and a tabu search that assigns operations to machines and
sequences them. The assignment and the sequence are coupled: a machine choice
changes the order on that machine and the completion time of every later
operation.

Ulusoy and Bilge~\cite{ulusoy1993} scheduled machines and automated guided
vehicles together. Their model shows that the transport decision changes the
start time of every operation, so the two schedules cannot be fixed
independently. Homayouni and Fontes~\cite{homayouni2021} study production and
transport scheduling in flexible job shop manufacturing systems. Yao et
al.~\cite{yao2026cp} formulate the AGV and machine integrated problem as a
constraint program. Dong et al.~\cite{dong2026handling} study joint
scheduling of production and material handling under a customized
manufacturing paradigm. Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025gphh} use a genetic
programming hyper-heuristic for an integrated order batching and picker
routing problem.

Exact and metaheuristic methods for parallel and batch machines are also
relevant. Zheng et al.~\cite{zheng2025formulations} formulate
parallel-batching scheduling with a collision-path model and valid
inequalities, and~\cite{zheng2025biobjective} give an exact bi-objective
approach for non-identical batch machines. Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2026upms} give a discrete particle swarm with dynamic
programming for unrelated parallel machines. Xu et
al.~\cite{xu2026autoclave} schedule autoclave molding with two-dimensional
packing and time windows, and Ding et al.~\cite{ding2024molding} add a
material exposure time window.

Energy and transport time enter the objective in several recent models. Yuan
et al.~\cite{yuan2025green} consider transportation time and machine
multi-rotation speeds in green flexible job shop scheduling. Cheng et
al.~\cite{cheng2025energy} solve an energy-saving flexible job shop problem
with multiple AGVs. Tang et al.~\cite{tang2025lotstreaming} integrate
lot-streaming with AGV transport in a hybrid flow shop. Zhu et
al.~\cite{zhu2026dfjspht} study a distributed flexible job shop with
heterogeneous transportation resources. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2026amr}
study production-logistics cooperation in a two-stage assembly flow shop
with a deteriorating robotic arm. Huang et al.~\cite{huang2025collaborative}
solve collaborative production and logistics scheduling with limited
transport resources and charging constraints. Chen et
al.~\cite{chen2025carseq} integrate car sequencing with vehicle routing on a
mixed-model assembly line, and~\cite{chen2026benders} give Benders
decompositions for order acceptance and scheduling in additive
manufacturing. Xu and Xie~\cite{xu2025forest} plan rescue routes with an ant
colony algorithm.

\subsection{Deep reinforcement learning for scheduling}

Deep reinforcement learning constructs a schedule step by step. Li et
al.~\cite{li2025tsmc} train a multi-agent policy for real-time scheduling of
a flexible job shop with AGVs and use an action-decoding step to reduce the
action space. Li et al.~\cite{li2025plcsp} apply multi-agent DRL to a
production-logistics collaborative environment, and Li et
al.~\cite{li2025jms} use a multi-agent system with DRL for self-organizing
scheduling. Shi et al.~\cite{shi2025nested} use a nested-hierarchical DRL
policy for production-logistics collaborative scheduling in dynamic flexible
job shops. Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025hierarchical} use hierarchical
multi-agent DRL for dynamic flexible job shop scheduling with transport.

Other work targets specific shop structures. Wu et al.~\cite{wu2026madappo}
study multi-agent collaborative reinforcement learning under random machine
failures. Geng et al.~\cite{geng2025dtdrl} combine a digital twin with
multi-agent DRL under a transport resource constraint. Yuan et
al.~\cite{yuan2026workers} schedule workers and AGVs hierarchically in a
distributed flexible job shop. Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025multitarget} learn
an end-to-end policy for a multitarget flexible job shop. Zhu et
al.~\cite{zhu2025matrix} apply a graph neural network and multi-action DRL
to matrix manufacturing layout and scheduling. Dong et
al.~\cite{dong2025heuristic} combine a heuristic with DRL for flexible job
shop scheduling under transport constraints. Lee et al.~\cite{lee2026digitaltwin}
use digital-twin-driven DRL for dynamic AGV systems, and Yi et
al.~\cite{yi2026petri} drive DRL with Petri-net heuristics for robotic job
shops.

Graph-based encoders are common in this line. Xu et
al.~\cite{taagnet2026} propose a graph-based multi-agent framework for
integrated production and AGV scheduling in a dynamic hybrid flow shop. Yang
et al.~\cite{yang2026hgamppo} combine heterogeneous graph attention with
multi-policy PPO for AGV-assisted flexible job shop scheduling. Yue et
al.~\cite{yue2027hchgnn} use a hub-centric heterogeneous graph for AGV
dispatching with unrelated parallel machines and finite buffers. Gao et
al.~\cite{gao2026gnntransformer} use a hybrid GNN--Transformer for AGV
scheduling in automated container terminals. Zhang et
al.~\cite{zhang2025sgformer} use a simplified graph transformer for
large-scale fuzzy flexible job shop scheduling.

Transformer-based and learning-to-improve methods are a related line. Xiao
et al.~\cite{xiao2026resched} use a transformer architecture with simplified
states for flexible job shop scheduling. Smit et
al.~\cite{smit2026dcan,smit2025stochastic} study neural multi-objective and
stochastic variants of the flexible job shop problem. Wang et
al.~\cite{wang2025memory} learn memory-enhanced improvement heuristics. Link
et al.~\cite{link2026coordination} analyse the coordination gap between
joint and modular learning for job shop scheduling with transport resources.

\subsection{Sequence models and set encoders}

The Transformer~\cite{vaswani2017} maps a sequence of tokens to contextual
representations with self-attention. The Set Transformer~\cite{lee2019set}
removes the order assumption and maps a set of tokens to a permutation-
invariant representation. Pointer networks~\cite{vinyals2015pointer} output
a permutation of the input set, and POMO~\cite{kwon2020pomo} optimizes
several policies from different starting points. Decision
Transformer~\cite{chen2021decision} casts reinforcement learning as sequence
modeling over trajectories. A set encoder fits the candidate sets in this
problem: the number of eligible machines and the number of available AGVs
change at every step.

\subsection{Policy optimization and credit assignment}

Proximal policy optimization (PPO)~\cite{schulman2017ppo} clips the policy
ratio and uses a value critic. Group-relative policy optimization
(GRPO)~\cite{shao2024grpo} removes the critic: a group of rollouts on the
same input provides a baseline, and the advantage is the within-group
z-score. GRPO was introduced for language-model reasoning, where a group of
responses to one prompt is natural. A scheduling episode also admits a
group: several rollouts on the same instance batch share a scalar return.

Cooperative multi-agent methods use a centralized critic during training.
Yu et al.~\cite{yu2022mappo} show that PPO with a centralized critic is a
strong baseline in cooperative multi-agent games. QMIX~\cite{rashid2018qmix}
factorizes a joint value function under a monotonicity constraint. When
several policy heads act jointly, the team reward must be split among them.
Counterfactual multi-agent policy gradients (COMA)~\cite{foerster2018coma}
use a joint-action Q-function and subtract the value of the other heads'
actions, averaged over the head's own policy. The Q-function must generalize
to actions that were not taken, which is difficult when each episode
contains few joint decisions.

Multi-objective and constrained formulations offer other ways to handle the
trade-off. MGDA~\cite{sener2018mgda} treats multi-task learning as
multi-objective optimization, and PCGrad~\cite{yu2020pcgrad} projects
conflicting gradients. Constrained policy
optimization~\cite{achiam2017cpo} handles constraints with a trust region,
and reward constrained policy optimization~\cite{tessler2019rcpo} penalizes
constraint violations during training. Hu et al.~\cite{hu2023constrained}
apply constrained RL to dynamic material handling. Reward shaping preserves
the optimal policy when the shaping term is potential-based~\cite{ng1999shaping}.

\subsection{Benchmarks and evaluation}

Recent work questions whether reported gains transfer across instances and
shops. Cao et al.~\cite{cao2026dynaschedbench} introduce a calibrated dynamic
scheduling benchmark. Jiang et al.~\cite{jiang2026atlas} provide a
learning-augmented dataset and benchmark, and Feng et
al.~\cite{feng2026frontierco} evaluate machine-learning solvers at
real-world scale. Other work learns from improvement heuristics or
self-labeling~\cite{zhang2024improvement,corsini2024selflabeling}, removes
the baseline~\cite{sepulveda2026baselinefree}, adapts per
instance~\cite{qing2026instancewise}, or optimizes several policies
together~\cite{choi2025multipolicy}. These studies set the evaluation
standard that we follow: a fixed test set, several training seeds, and a
baseline that is tuned on the same objective.

\subsection{Position of this work}

The works above cover the individual elements: flexible job shop scheduling,
AGV transport, attention encoders, and group-relative or counterfactual
policy optimization. We study their combination in a shop with several
workshops and one vehicle fleet per workshop. Three heads make the operation,
machine, and AGV decisions at the same step; the heads share a group-relative
advantage and use no critic; a top-level policy selects the rule prior every
few steps. The ablations isolate the encoder, the algorithm, and the AGV
head. The rule selector is evaluated against the same controller without it.
