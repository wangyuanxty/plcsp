\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper focuses on manufacturing plants with multiple workshops, such as those for automotive components 
or 3C electronics assembly. In such flexible job-shop multi-workshop scheduling problem, 
each workshop contains multiple machines, and workpieces are transferred between workshops 
by the respective AGV fleets through loading/unloading stations. After a batch of workpieces enters the system, 
each workpiece consists of a fixed sequence of multiple operations, and each operation has different 
candidate machines as well as different processing times and power consumption. 
Scheduling tasks are advanced through a step-by-step decision-making process: operation, machine, vehicle. 
At each step, one operation is selected from all schedulable operations whose previous operations have been completed, 
and a feasible machine is assigned to it. If the operation is not in the same workshop or not on the same machine 
as its previous operation, an AGV will be selected from the fleet of the relevant workshop to handle the transportation. 
For transfers within the same workshop, the local AGV first travels empty to the pickup machine and then 
delivers the loaded workpiece. For cross-workshop transfers, the transportation is completed in segments 
through the pickup machine, the source workshop loading/unloading station, the destination workshop 
loading/unloading station, and the destination machine. This process is repeated until a complete schedule is obtained. 
The objective is to minimize the makespan while reducing the weighted sum of processing and idle energy consumption 
and AGV loaded transportation time.

We propose a hierarchical multi-agent model for joint production and logistics scheduling in a flexible job shop 
with several workshops. Four agents carry it: a rule selector sets a rule prior every few steps, 
and three shop agents make the operation, machine, and AGV decisions. Each shop agent encodes its own candidates 
with a Set Transformer. The shop agents are trained with a shared group-relative advantage without critic. 
Consequently, in this scenario, it adapts better to changes in workshop conditions than fixed dispatching rules, 
and provides more accurate attribution than staged learning methods. Furthermore, for large-scale instances, 
it can—within seconds rather than minutes—achieve objective values that are $19\%$–$50\%$ lower than 
those produced by rule-based methods or DRL baselines, and which also outperform the results obtained 
via genetic algorithms.

From the perspective of production logistics scheduling managers, the following management strategies are summarized: 
search-based approaches are better suited to small-scale job shops with static, one-off schedules, 
while policy-based approaches are better suited to large-scale job shops and to dynamic settings where 
disruptions and changing priorities necessitate frequent rescheduling. Future work will focus on extending 
the proposed joint scheduling framework to cross-enterprise supply chain coordination and integrating it 
with organizational decision-making processes, so as to further enhance the managerial decision-making value of the 
dynamic scheduling method.
