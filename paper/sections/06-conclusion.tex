\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a hierarchical multi-agent controller for joint production and
logistics scheduling in a flexible job shop with several workshops. Three
heads make the operation, machine, and AGV decisions; a dual Set Transformer
encodes the candidates; the heads are trained with a shared group-relative
advantage and no critic; a top-level policy selects a rule prior every few
steps.

The experiments show that the encoder and the multi-actor update each improve
over their respective baselines, and that the controller is fast enough for
interactive use. The controller is not the best method in objective value: the
random-key genetic algorithm and the two-stage mixed-integer baseline return
better schedules when they are given more computation, and the advantage of
the controller is run time. The top-level rule selector has a small and uneven
effect. The action analysis shows that it is not a fixed rule: the machine
head moves from minimum energy to minimum transport, and the AGV head from
earliest free to nearest, as the partial schedule fills. At the larger size the
selector and the learned transport decision do not change the objective.

The transport model assigns AGVs to workshops and splits a cross-workshop trip
into two legs with a fixed handover delay. Charging, failures, and load and
unload times are outside the model. The mixed-integer baseline is a two-stage
heuristic, not a joint optimum. Extending the model to charging and failures,
and solving the joint problem on larger instances, are the next steps.
