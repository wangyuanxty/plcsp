\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper focuses on manufacturing plants with multiple workshops, such as those for automotive components or 3C
electronics assembly. In such flexible job-shop multi-workshop scheduling problem, 
each workshop contains multiple machines, and workpieces are transferred between workshops by 
the respective AGV fleets through loading/unloading stations. The objective is to minimize the makespan while
reducing the weighted sum of processing and idle energy consumption and AGV transportation cost.

We propose a hierarchical multi-agent model for joint production and logistics scheduling in a flexible job shop 
with several workshops. Four agents carry it: a rule selector sets a rule prior every few steps, 
and three shop agents make the operation, machine, and AGV decisions. Each shop agent encodes its own
candidates with a Set Transformer. The shop agents are trained with a shared group-relative advantage without critic. 
Consequently, in this scenario, it adapts better to changes in workshop conditions than fixed dispatching rules, 
and provides more accurate attribution than staged learning methods.

Across five test instance families, HMAGRA achieved lower mean objective
values than dispatching rules and reduced the mean objective by about
$19\%$--$37\%$ relative to the best-performing DRL baseline in each family.
The random-key genetic algorithm achieved lower mean objective values in
the two smaller families, whereas HMAGRA achieved values about
$16\%$--$43\%$ lower in the three larger families. In the runtime tests,
HMAGRA constructed schedules in seconds. The machine-side Set Transformer,
within-machine attention, group-relative update, and high-level rule selector
each reduced the mean objective relative to its corresponding ablation control.

For schedulers, genetic search remains a competitive option for smaller
instances when time is available for search. HMAGRA offers an alternative
when a schedule is needed quickly. The same trained policy also constructs
schedules under different tested objective weights without retraining. The
repair experiment examined how the schedule objective changes when a machine
requires repair during schedule construction. Future work will focus on
extending the proposed joint scheduling framework to cross-enterprise supply chain coordination and 
integrating it with organizational decision-making processes, 
so as to further enhance the managerial decision-making value of the dynamic scheduling method.
