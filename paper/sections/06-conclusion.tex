\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a hierarchical multi-agent controller for joint production and
logistics scheduling in a flexible job shop with several workshops. Three
agents make the operation, machine, and AGV decisions; a dual Set Transformer
encodes the candidates; the agents are trained with a shared group-relative
advantage and no critic; a top-level policy selects a rule prior every few
steps.

Our experiments show that the encoder and the multi-actor update each improve
over their respective baselines, and that our controller is fast enough for
interactive use. Its objective is lower than that of the fixed dispatching
rules and of the adapted DRL baselines at every size. Against the search
methods the outcome depends on the size. At $6\times6$ RGA and the
mixed-integer model return better schedules; at $10\times6$ the joint MIP
does, but the paired test does not separate the controller from RGA; at the
largest size the controller is ahead of RGA but 32.4\% above the joint MIP. That trade,
search time for a single pass, is the operating point of the method. Rule
selection has a small and uneven effect. Rule-usage analysis shows that the
selector is
not a fixed rule: as the partial schedule fills, the machine rule head moves
from minimum processing time to minimum ready time, the AGV rule head from
earliest arrival to nearest, and the job rule head from least work remaining
to earliest finish. At $10\times6$ the selector's mean effect falls to 1.3\%,
while the learned AGV head is still better than the best fixed rule by
7.3\%.

The transport model assigns AGVs to workshops and moves a cross-workshop
job through the two load/unload stations. Charging, failures, and load and
unload times are outside the model. The mixed-integer baseline is joint over
the transport legs and the AGV routes given the machine assignment. It closes
on all 100 instances at $6\times6$ and on 92 and 91 at the two larger sizes;
the others return a feasible schedule or nothing within the cap. Machine
assignment
is taken from the no-AGV model and held fixed, and a variant that leaves it
free does not close at these sizes. Next we want to scale that model and to
add charging and failures.
