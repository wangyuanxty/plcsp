\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a hierarchical multi-agent controller for joint production and
logistics scheduling in a flexible job shop with several workshops. Three
heads make the operation, machine, and AGV decisions; a dual Set Transformer
encodes the candidates; the heads are trained with a shared group-relative
advantage and no critic; a top-level policy selects a rule prior every few
steps.

The experiments show that the encoder and the multi-actor update each improve
over their respective baselines, and that the controller is fast enough for
interactive use. Its objective is lower than that of the fixed dispatching
rules and of the adapted DRL baselines at every size. Against the search
methods the outcome depends on the size: they return better schedules on the
smaller instances, and on the largest the controller is ahead. That trade,
search time for a single pass, is the operating point of the method. The
top-level rule
selector has a small and uneven effect. The action analysis shows that it is
not a fixed rule: the machine head moves from minimum energy to minimum
transport, and the AGV head from earliest free to nearest, as the partial
schedule fills. At the larger size the selector and the learned transport
decision do not change the objective.

The transport model assigns AGVs to workshops and moves a cross-workshop
job through the two load/unload stations. Charging, failures, and load and
unload times are outside the model. The mixed-integer baseline is a two-stage
heuristic, not a joint optimum. Extending the model to charging and failures,
and solving the joint problem on larger instances, are the next steps.
