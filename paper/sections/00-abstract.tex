Flexible job shops with several workshops and one vehicle fleet per workshop
must decide, for every operation, when it starts, on which machine it runs,
and which vehicle moves it. The three decisions are coupled, and an energy-aware objective makes idle
machines and empty vehicle travel matter even when they do not change the
makespan. We present a controller with three policy heads. The operation and
machine heads use a dual Set Transformer; the machine encoder groups its
features into three views and applies attention within a machine before
attention across machines. The low-level heads are trained with a shared
group-relative advantage and no value critic. A top-level policy selects a
rule prior for each head every few steps. The controller builds a schedule in a single pass
and is fast enough for interactive use. It is not the best method in
objective value: a genetic algorithm and a mixed-integer baseline return
better schedules when they are given more computation. The contribution is a
multi-agent controller that makes the production and transport decisions
together, at near-interactive speed. The encoder and the multi-actor update
improve over their baselines, and the controller is compared with rule-based,
metaheuristic, mixed-integer, and published DRL baselines.
