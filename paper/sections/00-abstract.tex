In modern manufacturing systems, especially in multi-workshop production environments, 
automated guided vehicles (AGVs) play a critical role in transporting jobs between machines and workshops.
Existing flexible job shop scheduling studies largely decouple processing and transportation decisions, 
making it difficult to jointly optimize machine availability, energy consumption, 
and makespan under dynamically varying candidate action sets and shared rewards. Consequently, 
rule-based methods lack adaptability, while staged learning-based methods cannot accurately 
attribute the contribution of each decision.
To address these challenges, we build a multi-agent model that makes decisions
in one step with four agents: a rule selector and three shop actors. Each of
the three actors encodes its own candidates with a Set Transformer. Training
of the low-level agents relies on a shared group-relative advantage and omits
the critic, and a higher-level policy periodically assigns a rule prior to
each agent. The weights are an input, so one     policy covers the weight surface
without retraining. It achieves objective values $19\%$ to $45\%$ lower than
those of the rule-based dispatch and the adapted DRL baselines across all
problem sizes, and also outperforms the genetic algorithm on large instances,
finding solutions in seconds rather than minutes. Ablation experiments
demonstrate the effectiveness of the Set Transformer encoders, the critic-free
update, and the hierarchical rule selector.
