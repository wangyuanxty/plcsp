In a flexible job shop with several workshops, each served by its own fleet of
vehicles, every operation needs a start time, a machine, and a vehicle, and the
three choices are coupled because a machine that finishes early can wait for a
vehicle while a busy vehicle delays the next operation. The objective weighs
makespan, energy, and transport time. Learning the three decisions together is
difficult: the candidate sets change size at every step, and the three
decisions share one return. We build a controller that
makes the three decisions in one step with four agents: a rule selector and
three shop actors. Each of the three actors encodes its own candidates with a
Set Transformer. We train the low-level agents with a shared group-relative
advantage and no value critic, and a top-level policy picks a rule prior for
each agent every few steps. One pass produces a schedule, fast enough to use
interactively. It returns an objective 19 to 45\% lower than rule-based
dispatch and than the adapted DRL baselines at every size. Against a
random-key genetic algorithm
the controller gives up ground only in the smallest shop; in the largest it is
ahead, in seconds against minutes of search. In ablations, replacing the Set
Transformer machine encoder and replacing the critic-free update each cost
more than the rule selector returns.
