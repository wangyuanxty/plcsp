Flexible job shop scheduling is a core problem in modern manufacturing,
especially in automotive parts production, 3C electronics assembly, and
semiconductor wafer fabrication. These plants typically consist of multiple
workshops, and automated guided vehicles (AGVs) are widely used to move jobs
between them, so transport becomes part of scheduling. In these settings,
machine availability, energy consumption, and makespan all depend on how
processing and transportation resources are coordinated. However, existing
work rarely considers these aspects together. Making the three decisions
jointly is difficult. Rule-based methods commit to one dispatching policy and
cannot adapt to the shop state. Many learning-based methods separate the
decisions into stages. At each step the candidate sets change size, and the
three decisions share one return, which a per-agent critic cannot attribute.
To address these challenges, we build a multi-agent model that makes decisions
in one step with four agents: a rule selector and three shop actors. Each of
the three actors encodes its own candidates with a Set Transformer. Training
of the low-level agents relies on a shared group-relative advantage and omits
the critic, and a higher-level policy periodically assigns a rule prior to
each agent. The weights are an input, so one policy covers the weight surface
without retraining. It achieves objective values $19\%$ to $45\%$ lower than
those of the rule-based dispatch and the adapted DRL baselines across all
problem sizes, and also outperforms the genetic algorithm on large instances,
finding solutions in seconds rather than minutes. Ablation experiments
demonstrate the effectiveness of the Set Transformer encoders, the critic-free
update, and the hierarchical rule selector.
