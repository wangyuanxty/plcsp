Flexible job shops with several workshops and one vehicle fleet per workshop
must decide, for every operation, when it starts, on which machine it runs,
and which vehicle moves it. The three decisions are coupled, and an
energy-aware objective charges idle machines even when they do not change the
makespan. We present a controller with three policy heads. The operation and
machine heads use a dual Set Transformer; the machine encoder groups its
features into three views and applies attention within a machine before
attention across machines. The low-level heads are trained with a shared
group-relative advantage and no value critic. A top-level policy selects a
rule prior for each head every few steps. The controller builds a schedule in
a single pass and is fast enough for interactive use. It returns a lower
objective than rule-based dispatch and than adapted DRL baselines. Against
the search methods the result depends on the instance size: they are better
on the smaller instances, and on the largest the controller is ahead of both.
The contribution is the operating point: a good schedule in one pass, at any
size. The encoder and the multi-actor update also improve over their
baselines.
